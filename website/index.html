On the bike

Switzerland has a signposted network of national cycle routes, from flat lakeside paths to some of the most famous climbs in the Alps.

The three passes loop

Central Alps, around Grimsel, Furka and Susten

Three high Alpine passes linked into one big loop. Long climbs, stacked hairpins, and glacier views at the top. A serious day out that rewards good legs and an early start.

  • Grimsel2,164 m
  • Furka2,429 m
  • Susten2,224 m

Lakes Route

National cycle route 9, Montreux to Rorschach

Strings together a chain of Swiss lakes from Lake Geneva to Lake Constance. Gentler than the passes, with plenty of chances to stop for a swim.

Rhine Route

National cycle route 2, Andermatt to Basel

Follows the Rhine from the high Alps down to Basel. Mostly downhill in that direction, with gorges, vineyards and old towns along the river.

On foot

Yellow signposts, mountain huts, and trains that run right up into the valleys make Switzerland one of the easiest places in the world to go hiking.

Oeschinensee

Kandersteg, Bernese Oberland

A turquoise lake below steep rock walls. Take the gondola and a short walk, or climb higher for the view down onto the water.

Five Lakes Walk

Zermatt, Valais

Five small mountain lakes in a row. On a calm morning, several of them mirror the Matterhorn.

Aletsch Glacier

Valais

The longest glacier in the Alps and part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The ridge trails above it show just how huge it is.

Reading the trail

Swiss trails are colour-coded by difficulty. Check the marker before you set off, not halfway up.

Hiking trailYellow. Suitable for most walkers in normal shoes or light boots.
Mountain hiking trailWhite, red, white. Steep and narrow in places. You need sure footing and a head for heights.
Alpine routeWhite, blue, white. May cross snow, scree or rock without a path. Mountaineering experience required.

Good to know

  • Signposts show walking times, not distances. Times assume a steady pace without breaks.
  • Bikes ride on most Swiss trains with a bike ticket or bike day pass, so you can ride one way and train back.
  • Start early in summer. Afternoon thunderstorms build quickly in the mountains.
  • SwitzerlandMobility maps the national hiking and cycling routes, which helps with planning.
